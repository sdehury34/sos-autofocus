Speed-of-sound autofocus: does the delay model matter?

A scanner fires steered plane waves with delays computed for 1540 m/s. This demo simulates a medium with a speed of sound you choose, then estimates that speed back from the echoes with two delay models.

Conventional: keeps the steering angle fixed (p = sinθ / c)
Transmit-delay-consistent: keeps the slowness of the firing delays (p = sinθ / c0)

In this noise-free simulation, the point targets become sharp at the right speed. In real tissue the difference is much harder to see, but the estimated speed, the biomarker, still changes.

What am I seeing?

  • Focus curve. For each trial speed, the echoes are beamformed and the coherence factor (how well the echoes of all 128 elements line up) is measured. The estimate is the speed with the highest value.
  • Try it. Move the true speed far from 1540 m/s. The conventional estimate stays closer to 1540 m/s; the consistent estimate follows the true speed. Near 1540 m/s both models are the same.
  • Data. Simulated 128-element linear array, 5 MHz, 31 plane waves (±15°), speckle with a cyst and four point targets. All results were computed with the code of the paper and stored in this page.
  • In vivo. On 62 calf scans with a through-transmission reference, the consistent model decreases the error from 27.6 to 12.5 m/s.
